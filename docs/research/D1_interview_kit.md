## 1. Recruiting screener

### 1.1 Who qualifies — the "connector" adult

A participant qualifies if **all** of these are true:

| # | Criterion | Screener question that tests it |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Age 25–50 | Q1 |
| C2 | Lives in a **different city or country** from at least one close family member (parent, sibling, spouse during a long-distance phase, or adult child) | Q2 |
| C3 | Self-describes as the person who **coordinates** family matters — remembers dates, checks on parents, organizes gifts/visits, relays news between members | Q3 (must pick ≥2 of the listed activities) |
| C4 | Is in touch with family at least **weekly** (calls, WhatsApp, or similar) | Q4 |
| C5 | Personally pays for at least one digital subscription (any — Netflix, Spotify, iCloud, etc.) | Q5 (proxy for being a realistic payer; not a WTP signal) |

**Disqualifiers:** works in market research or for a journaling/family-app
company; immediate family member of the Aangan team (friends-of-friends are
acceptable for a student pilot — mark them `warm-contact` in the tracker and
report the share in the plan).

### 1.2 Quotas (for n = 15; scale proportionally for 10–20)

| Segment | Quota | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| India-resident connector | 8–9 | Mix metro (Bengaluru/Mumbai/Delhi-NCR) + tier-2 |
| NRI connector (parents in India) | 5–6 | Prioritize US/UK/UAE/Canada/Singapore corridors |
| Of the total: women | ≥ 6 | Connector role is often gendered; do not sample only men |
| Of the total: caring for a parent 60+ | ≥ 5 | The elder-signal use case must be represented |
| Of the total: Hindi as primary family language | ≥ 5 | Core persona; interview may be run in Hinglish |

### 1.3 Screener form (send as a 2-minute Google Form)

1. Your age: `<25 / 25–34 / 35–50 / >50` — *qualify: 25–34 or 35–50*
2. Do any of these close family members live in a different city or country
   from you? (tick all): `parent / sibling / spouse-partner / adult child / none`
   — *qualify: any except "none"*
3. In your family, which of these do **you** usually end up doing? (tick all):
   `remembering birthdays & occasions / checking in on parents' health /
   organizing gifts / planning visits & travel / passing news between family
   members / none of these` — *qualify: ≥2 ticked*
4. How often are you in touch with family who live elsewhere?
   `daily / few times a week / weekly / monthly / rarely` — *qualify: weekly+*
5. Do you personally pay for any app or streaming subscription? `yes / no`
   — *qualify: yes*
6. Where do you live? `city, country` — *for quota routing*
7. Which language does your family mostly use with each other? `free text`
8. Would you be open to a 45-minute recorded video/phone conversation about
   how your family stays in touch? `yes / no` + contact details.

**Incentive:** state it up front and keep it flat (e.g., ₹500 / $10 gift
voucher, or none for warm contacts — record which in the tracker).

## 2. Semi-structured interview guide (45 minutes)

Format: one interviewer + one note-taker if possible; video call; record with
consent (Section 3 script first). Ask questions **exactly as worded** where
wording is given; probes are optional follow-ups. Never suggest an answer,
never mention journaling, AI, or Aangan before the debrief.

### Block 0 — Consent & warm-up (5 min)

Run the consent script (Section 3). Then:

- W1. "To start, tell me a little about your family — who's where?"
- W2. "Walk me through how you stayed in touch with them over the last week."
  - Probe: "Who reached out to whom?" · "Was any of that a routine, like a
    fixed call time?"

### Block 1 — Critical incident: "the last time you missed something" (12 min)

The core evidence for Section 2. Get **specific, dated stories**, not
generalities. If the participant speaks in generalities, ask for "the most
recent time" until you get an actual event.

- Q1. "Tell me about the **last time you found out late** about something going
  on with a family member — something you wish you'd known earlier."
  - Probe: "When was this?" · "How did you eventually find out?" · "How much
    later was 'late'?" · "What happened because you didn't know sooner?"
  - Probe (feelings, neutral): "What was that like for you?"
- Q2. "Has there been a time when you missed a **hint** someone dropped — a
  gift wish, a preference, something they wanted?"
  - Probe: "What did you end up doing instead?" · "Did they say anything about it?"
- Q3. "And the other direction — a time when something was going on with
  **you** that your family found out about late, or never?"
  - Probe: "Was that deliberate on your part, or did it just happen?"
- Q4. "How often would you say something like these stories happens in your
  family?" (Let them estimate in their own words — do not offer a scale.)

> Interviewer note: capture each incident as WHO / WHAT was missed / DELAY /
> CONSEQUENCE / EMOTION — this becomes a row in the incident table (Section 5).

### Block 2 — Current workarounds (8 min)

- Q5. "You mentioned [WhatsApp group / calls / …]. Walk me through how the
  family WhatsApp group actually gets used — what gets posted there, and what
  doesn't?"
  - Probe: "What kinds of things would someone in your family **never** put in
    the group?" · "What happens to important things posted there — do they get
    acted on, or scroll away?"
- Q6. "When you want to know how your mother/father is *really* doing, what do
  you actually do?"
  - Probe: "How well does that work?" · "What does it cost you — time, timing,
    awkwardness?"
- Q7. "Do you use anything to remember family dates, gift ideas, health
  details — apps, notes, your memory?"
  - Probe: "When did that system last fail you?"
- Q8. "If you've ever tried an app *for the family* — location sharing, shared
  albums, a family calendar — how did that go?"
  - Probe: "Who in the family refused or dropped off, and why?"

### Block 3 — Privacy attitudes inside the family (8 min)

Tests the consent-gradient hypothesis without proposing it.

- Q9. "Is there information you'd want your family to know **only if it became
  important** — but not day-to-day?"
  - Probe: "Can you give me an example category — health, money, feelings,
    plans?" · "Who exactly would you want to know, and who not?"
- Q10. "Think about your parents: do they share differently with you than with
  your siblings, or with their friends?"
  - Probe: "How do you find out the things they *don't* tell you directly?"
- Q11. "Some families use location-sharing apps to keep an eye on each other.
  How would that go down in your family?"
  - Probe: "Where's the line between caring and checking-up in your family?"
- Q12. "If a family member kept a private diary and something in it mattered to
  you — say a health worry — what do you feel *should* happen?"
  (Open; note whether they spontaneously invent consent-based sharing.)

### Block 4 — Willingness-to-pay ladder (8 min)

Order is mandatory: **problem value → unprompted price → Van Westendorp band →
anchored ladder.** Read the concept card verbatim; it is deliberately
feature-thin and never framed as "our product".

> **Concept card (read aloud):** "Imagine a service for your whole family. Each
> member privately records small everyday updates in their own words — by
> voice, in their own language. Each person controls exactly what, if
> anything, is shared, and with whom. Family members can ask it questions —
> like what a parent has been mentioning lately, or what someone might want as
> a gift — and it answers only from what people chose to share, and can nudge
> you when something a family member flagged as important comes up."

- Q13. "What's your honest first reaction?" (Do not defend the concept. Note
  objections verbatim — these feed Section 4 persona objections.)
- Q14. "Who in your family would use it, and who wouldn't? Why?"
- Q15. **Unprompted WTP:** "If this existed for your whole family, what would
  you expect it to cost per month — as one family plan?" (Silence until they
  give a number. Record currency.)
- Q16. **Van Westendorp (neutral by construction), same family-plan-per-month
  framing:**
  - "At what price would it be **so cheap** you'd doubt the quality?"
  - "At what price would it be **a bargain**?"
  - "At what price would it start to feel **expensive**, but you'd still consider it?"
  - "At what price would it be **too expensive** to consider at all?"
- Q17. **Anchored ladder** — "I'll read a few price points; for each, tell me:
  definitely not / probably not / maybe / probably / definitely."
  - India-resident, per family per month: **₹199 → ₹299 → ₹499 → ₹799**
  - NRI, per family per month: **$2.99 → $4.99 → $7.99 → $9.99**
  - Read in ascending order for half the participants and descending for the
    other half (mark which in the tracker, to control anchoring direction).
  - Probe after their band flips negative: "What would have to be true for
    [rejected price] to feel fair?"
- Q18. "What do you currently pay for family-shared subscriptions —
  Netflix/Spotify/iCloud family, anything?" (Reference frame, asked **after**
  the ladder so it can't anchor it.)

### Block 5 — Closing & debrief (4 min)

- Q19. "If you could change one thing about how your family stays in touch,
  what would it be?" (Their words → value-proposition language for Section 7.)
- Q20. "Is there anything I should have asked about but didn't?"
- Q21. "Would you be open to trying an early version with your family and
  telling us what breaks?" (Pilot pipeline for D2/Section 20 — record yes/no.)
- **Debrief:** reveal that the team is building such a product ("Aangan"),
  restate that nothing they said obligates them, re-confirm recording consent
  now that they know the affiliation, and thank them. Log the incentive.

## 3. Consent script (read before recording starts)

> "Thanks for making time. Before we start, a few things:
>
> 1. **Who we are.** We're a student team researching how families who live
>    apart stay in touch. This conversation is for a business-plan research
>    project.
> 2. **Recording.** I'd like to record audio of this call so I can transcribe
>    it accurately. Is that okay? You can say no and we'll do notes only.
> 3. **How we'll use it.** The recording and transcript are used only for this
>    research. In any report, you'll appear under a pseudonym — no name, no
>    employer, no identifying details. Direct quotes may be used, anonymized.
> 4. **Storage.** Recordings and transcripts are stored in a private drive
>    accessible only to the project team, and deleted within 12 months of the
>    project ending.
> 5. **Your rights.** You can skip any question, stop at any time, or — up to
>    the point our report is submitted — ask us to withdraw and delete your
>    data entirely, no reason needed. Contact: [email].
> 6. There are no right answers; honest and even negative reactions are the
>    most useful thing you can give us.
>
> Do you consent to take part, and to the recording? [wait for a clear yes]
> Thank you — starting the recording now, and I'll ask you to confirm consent
> once more on the recording."

Log in the tracker per participant: consent given (Y/N), recording (Y/N),
withdrawal requests (date, action taken).

## 4. Quantitative survey (Google-Forms-ready, 10–12 questions)

Purpose: frequency data for Section 2 ("how often") and segment sizing texture
for Section 4. Distribute beyond interviewees (target n ≥ 40): NRI community
groups, workplace groups, alumni networks. Keep the form anonymous; no product
mention anywhere; title it **"How families who live apart stay in touch — 3-minute survey."**

| # | Question (exact text) | Type / scale |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Do you have close family (parent, sibling, spouse, adult child) living in a different city or country from you? | Yes / No — **if No, end survey (screen-out)** |
| S2 | Which best describes you? | Single choice: I live in India / I live outside India with family in India / Other |
| S3 | Your age group | <25 / 25–34 / 35–44 / 45–54 / 55+ |
| S4 | In your family, are you usually the one who remembers occasions, checks on parents, and coordinates between members? | Yes, mostly me / Shared with someone / Someone else / No one really |
| S5 | How often do you find out **late** about a family member's small troubles (health niggles, low mood, everyday problems)? | Never / A few times a year / Monthly / Weekly / Almost daily |
| S6 | How often do you find out late about **positive** things (good news, wishes, things they wanted)? | Same 5-point scale as S5 |
| S7 | In the last 12 months, did finding out late lead to any of the following? (tick all) | A missed health signal I wish I'd caught / A missed occasion or gift opportunity / A family disagreement / Guilt or anxiety on my part / None of these |
| S8 | "I sometimes hold back from sharing things with my family because the group chat feels too public." | Likert 1–5 (Strongly disagree → Strongly agree) |
| S9 | "There are things about my parents' daily life I only learn when they become big problems." | Likert 1–5 |
| S10 | How many paid app/streaming subscriptions does your household currently have? | 0 / 1–2 / 3–5 / 6+ |
| S11 | Has your family ever paid for an app used by the whole family (storage, streaming family plan, safety app)? | Yes / No / Not sure |
| S12 | If a service noticeably reduced how often you miss things that matter in your family, what is the **most** your family would realistically pay per month for it? | Single choice — India form: Nothing / under ₹100 / ₹100–199 / ₹200–349 / ₹350–599 / ₹600–999 / ₹1,000+ · NRI form: Nothing / under $2 / $2–4 / $5–7 / $8–12 / $13+ |

Build notes: two form copies (India / NRI) differing only in S12 currency;
S1 uses Forms section-skip logic to end screen-outs politely; report screen-out
rate — it is itself a segment-prevalence data point.

## 5. Analysis plan

### 5.1 Interview coding workflow

1. **Transcribe** within 48h of each interview (auto-transcribe, then correct
   by hand; keep Hinglish as spoken — translate only in quotes used in the plan,
   marked "[translated]").
2. **Incident table first.** Extract every Block-1 story into a row:
   `participant / who was missed / what / delay / consequence / emotion /
   verbatim quote`. This table **is** the Section 2 evidence.
3. **Open coding** on the first 3 transcripts by two team members
   independently; merge into a codebook. Starter codes (extend, don't force):
   - `MISS-health` `MISS-hint` `MISS-event` — types of missed signals
   - `WA-noise` `WA-performative` `WA-nothing-remembered` — WhatsApp failure modes
   - `CALL-guilt` `CALL-sync-burden` — phone-call failure modes
   - `PRIV-selective` (share X with A but not B) `PRIV-surveillance-aversion`
     `PRIV-elder-modesty` — privacy gradient evidence
   - `WTP-value-anchor` (what they compared the price to) `WTP-payer-identity`
     (who they assume pays) `OBJ-*` (objections: another-app, elder-adoption,
     AI-trust, data-fear)
4. **Apply the codebook** to all transcripts. Second coder re-codes a 20%
   sample; discuss every disagreement; report agreement qualitatively
   ("codes with disagreement: …") — no need for kappa at this n.
5. **Theme = code cluster + ≥3 participants + ≥1 verbatim quote.** Themes
   supported by fewer than 3 participants go in the plan as "signals to
   validate", never as findings.
6. **Disconfirming-evidence rule:** for every theme, actively search
   transcripts for counter-examples and report them ("2 of 14 said group chat
   works fine for them"). The rubric rejects cherry-picking; this is the guard.

### 5.2 WTP analysis

- **Unprompted WTP (Q15):** report median and range, split India vs NRI. This
  is the headline number; the ladder only brackets it.
- **Van Westendorp (Q16):** with n=10–20 plot the four cumulative lines and
  report the acceptable band (point of marginal cheapness → point of marginal
  expensiveness) as a **range**, clearly labeled "directional, small-n".
- **Ladder (Q17):** per price point, % answering probably/definitely. Note
  ascending vs descending read order; if order visibly shifts answers, say so.
- **Survey S12:** distribution chart, split by form. Present alongside — not
  averaged with — interview WTP.
- Cross-check against Q18/S10–S11 reference prices (what families actually pay
  today) and state the comparison explicitly in Section 8.

### 5.3 Findings → business-plan section map

| Output | Feeds section | As |
|---|---|---|
| Incident table + frequency estimates (Q4, S5–S7) | **2** Problem | "X of N connectors reported a late-discovered health signal in the past year", with quotes |
| Persona details, objections (Q13–Q14, OBJ-* codes), channels | **4** Target market | Validated/corrected Aditya persona; real objection list |
| Segment prevalence texture (screener + S1 screen-out rate, S2–S4) | **5** Market analysis | Bottom-up sizing assumptions with stated basis; combined with Section 6 secondary sources |
| Workaround failure modes (WA-*, CALL-* codes) | **2, 6** Alternatives & competitors | Evidence that WhatsApp/calls are the real incumbent and where they fail |
| Privacy-gradient evidence (PRIV-* codes, Q9–Q12) | **2, 7, 14** | Demand-side evidence for consent-gradient as the differentiator |
| WTP band + payer identity | **8, 15** Business model & financials | Price candidates for the fake-door test and the D3 revenue model |
| Pilot volunteers (Q21) | **20** Recommendation | Named pipeline for the 3–5-family pilot |
| Everything above | **20** | Go/no-go evidence: proceed to pilot only if ≥60% of interviewees report a monthly-or-worse miss frequency AND median unprompted WTP ≥ the free-tier cost floor from D3 — state the thresholds before fieldwork and report against them |

## 6. Secondary-source checklist

Quality bar for **every** figure, per the rubric: named source + publication
year (≤3 years old preferred; older must be flagged) + currency stated + 
geography stated (India / corridor / global) + a working link archived in the
tracker. If a number can't meet the bar, the plan says "no reliable figure
found" — never an invented one.

| # | Exact figure needed | Feeds section | Acceptable source class | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | India DPDP Act 2023: current enforcement status, Rules notification status, consent-manager and data-fiduciary obligations relevant to intimate personal data | 5, 14 | MeitY / Gazette of India primary text; top-tier law-firm briefings (secondary) | Status changes fast — check within 30 days of submission |
| 2 | DPDP treatment of minors (verifiable parental consent, age threshold) | 14 | Same as #1 | Backs the 18+ MVP policy |
| 3 | Number of Indian-diaspora / overseas-Indian households, by corridor (US, UK, UAE, Canada, Singapore, Australia) | 4, 5 | MEA "Population of Overseas Indians" (latest release); UN DESA migrant-stock tables | TAM numerator for NRI segment |
| 4 | Annual remittance inflows to India, USD, latest FY | 5 | World Bank/KNOMAD brief; RBI bulletin | Proves spend + emotional link in the corridor; state FY and USD |
| 5 | India smartphone users and internet users, latest year | 5 | IAMAI-Kantar ICUBE; TRAI performance indicators | SAM denominator inputs |
| 6 | WhatsApp monthly active users in India (or best available proxy) | 2, 6 | Company statements; reputable business press citing them | Label as company-claimed if no primary figure |
| 7 | Journaling-app market size and growth rate (global; India if available) | 5 | Named market-research houses; triangulate ≥2, state both | Flag paywalled-abstract numbers as such |
| 8 | Family/parental-app or family-safety-app market size (Life360 revenue as floor proxy) | 5, 6 | Life360 public filings (primary); market reports | Public-company filings beat estimates |
| 9 | India consumer subscription benchmarks: current Netflix/Spotify/YouTube family-plan prices in INR | 8 | Provider price pages, screenshot-archived with date | Anchors the ₹199–799 ladder as market-real |
| 10 | Share of Indian elderly (60+) living apart from adult children; elder loneliness/contact-frequency data | 2, 4 | LASI (Longitudinal Ageing Study in India) wave reports; NSO "Elderly in India" | Strongest neutral evidence for the problem's scale |
| 11 | Indic-language internet-user share / preference for voice interfaces in India | 4, 7 | IAMAI; Google-KPMG Indic-languages reports (flag age if >3y) | Backs Hindi-voice-first design claims |
| 12 | Paid-subscription conversion benchmarks for consumer freemium apps (global) | 15 | RevenueCat "State of Subscription Apps" or equivalent published dataset | Labels the D3 conversion input as benchmark-based assumption |

geography / link / archived copy / quality flag (meets bar? Y/N) / used in section`.
